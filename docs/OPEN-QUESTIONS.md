# Open Questions

The seed defines a direction and a method. It does not yet provide a complete institutional design. Priority areas include:

- **Flourishing:** What should count as improvement, and how do we represent conflicting needs?
- **Rights:** Which protections constrain collective goals, and how are conflicts resolved?
- **Legitimate authority:** When may one person or group make binding decisions affecting others?
- **Power:** How is authority granted, bounded, audited, rotated, and removed?
- **Economics:** How should ownership, labour, essentials, dependency, and unequal bargaining power be addressed?
- **Knowledge:** How can expertise inform decisions without acquiring unchecked authority?
- **Conflict and repair:** How should disputes, accountability, restitution, and reintegration work?
- **Tribalism and pluralism:** How can institutions protect belonging and collective self-determination without producing exclusion, scapegoating, factional capture, or unequal citizenship? What helps people maintain multiple, overlapping identities?
- **Scale:** What belongs locally, and what requires coordination across communities?
- **Ecology and technology:** How should uncertain, widespread, or irreversible harms be evaluated?
- **Transition:** How can changes be tested without creating coercive emergency authority?
- **Failure and exit:** How can people replace institutions, and what happens to those unable to leave?
- **Culture and learning:** How can healthy habits and shared meaning develop while protecting pluralism?
- **Project governance:** Who may merge changes, what review is required, and how can maintainers be replaced?

These are invitations for research and proposals. Mark proposed answers as drafts until a documented process establishes otherwise.
